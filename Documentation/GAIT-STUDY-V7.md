# Original running footage and v7 run correction

The original running footage is now directly observed in moving video, unlike v5's walking-only evidence. [The Seattle Times video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RqlQYJUz-j8), “Meet Jimothy, Seattle’s internet-famous raccoon,” plays publicly in Brave without login. Its first roughly three seconds show Kiana Hall's grass-to-sidewalk running sequence, credited on screen. Later shots are porch movement and were not used as fast-run evidence.

Method: replayed the public player and captured its visible frames in sequence, first at about 2 captures/sec, then about 12.5 captures/sec. This was inspection of a moving-video frame sequence, not an inference from a lone running photo and not a claim of continuous human playback. Browser loading introduced duplicate/blank early frames; only visible running samples were used. Source and screenshots stay temporary, outside the repository and shipped game. Parent review sheet: `/tmp/jimothy-run-v7/detail-overview.png`; useful original screenshots are `detail/run-022.png` through `run-052.png`. Source pixels are modest resolution; far-side feet and some ground contacts are partly occluded.

Observed motion: a conspicuous foreleg reach forward; a downward catch followed by the compact body gathering over the planted forefeet; rear feet folding high behind/under the body and then sweeping down/back; an uneven pitch/recovery of the rump instead of a smooth uniform up/down bounce. Visible limbs are staggered rather than perfectly synchronized pairs. The short tail stays close to the rump. Exact left/right identity, contact duty fractions, real distance/speed and precise biological cycle frequency cannot be measured reliably from these views.

Access limitations: the original Instagram reel exposed by [FOX 13](https://www.fox13seattle.com/news/jimothy-seattle-racoon-goes-viral) opens a login/signup overlay; it was not bypassed. The article's inline image captioned as running is a JPEG, not a playable gait reference. YouTube text fetch was previously throttled, but native browser playback succeeded this pass. No downloaded stream extraction or access-control workaround was used.

`Tools/refine_gait_v7.py` exposes `refine(rig)` and replaces only Waddle after anatomy/rest changes and existing gait initialization. Idle, Walk and phase-driven Jump are preserved. The adaptation uses a pronounced fore reach/catch, quick forequarter compression, gathered hind recovery, downward/back kick and a longer uneven recovery. Fore contacts use authored phase offsets 0/.12 and hind .45/.52; these numerical values approximate the visible order and are not measured motion capture. Paws remain level in stance, curl only during recovery, and remain attached at the ankle. Analytic stance translation retains the same nominal speed.

Timing: 13 authored frames at24fps, period.5s, 97 sampled keys at eighth-frame spacing. Nominal armature speed stays9.75 units/s, therefore2.145m/s at.22 scale; the existing3m/s movement ratio is unchanged. This yields about2.8 cycles/sec at that runtime speed instead of the previous4.2, allowing the reach/gather/kick to read. The cadence is an authored visual adaptation, not a measured biological frequency. Export at .125frame step with simplification disabled to preserve all keys, or validate any reduced sampling separately.

Read-only validation against `Art/Blender/Jimothy-v7.blend` showed no reach clamping, solver error below6e-7 and joint gaps below5e-7. Four native poses were rendered and inspected: catch, gathered fore support, hind support and kick/recovery. No detached joints or inversion appeared. Loop/stance metrics are in `gait-v7-validation.json`. This agent does not export the final FBX or approve runtime gait from these Blender checks alone.
